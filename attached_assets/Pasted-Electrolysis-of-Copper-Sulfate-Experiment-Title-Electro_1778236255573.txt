Electrolysis of Copper Sulfate Experiment
Title
Electrolysis of Copper Sulfate Solution
Aim
To investigate the electrolysis of copper sulfate solution using copper electrodes and observe the products formed at the electrodes.
Apparatus and Materials
•	Beaker
•	Copper sulfate solution
•	Two copper electrodes
•	Battery or DC power supply
•	Connecting wires with crocodile clips
•	Ammeter 
•	Switch
•	Retort stand (optional)
Chemical Used
•	Copper sulfate solution (CuSO₄)
Theory
Electrolysis is the chemical decomposition of an ionic compound in molten or aqueous form by passing electric current through it.
Copper sulfate solution contains:
•	Copper ions (Cu²⁺)
•	Sulfate ions (SO₄²⁻)
When electric current passes through the solution:
•	Positive copper ions move to the cathode (negative electrode)
•	Negative sulfate ions move to the anode (positive electrode)
Using copper electrodes:
•	Copper is deposited at the cathode
•	Copper dissolves from the anode into the solution
Setup Diagram
A beaker containing copper sulfate solution with two copper electrodes connected to a DC power supply.
Procedure
1.	Pour copper sulfate solution into a beaker.
2.	Insert two clean copper electrodes into the solution.
3.	Connect the electrodes to a DC power supply:
o	Positive terminal to the anode
o	Negative terminal to the cathode
4.	Switch on the current and allow electrolysis to proceed for several minutes.
5.	Observe changes at both electrodes.
6.	Record all observations.
Observations
•	A reddish-brown deposit forms on the cathode.
•	The anode gradually becomes smaller.
•	The blue color of the solution remains almost unchanged.
Explanation of Observations

Overall Effect
•	Copper is transferred from the anode to the cathode.
•	Concentration of copper sulfate solution remains nearly constant.
Conclusion
During electrolysis of copper sulfate using copper electrodes:
•	Copper metal is deposited at the cathode.
•	Copper dissolves at the anode.
•	The mass of the cathode increases while the mass of the anode decreases.
Precautions
1.	Use clean copper electrodes.
2.	Ensure electrodes do not touch each other.
3.	Use a low DC voltage.
4.	Handle electrical equipment carefully.
5.	Avoid spilling copper sulfate solution.
Applications of Electrolysis of Copper Sulfate
1.	Electroplating
2.	Purification of copper
3.	Extraction of metals
4.	Industrial electrolysis processes
