Core Learning Goal

Students should intuitively realize:

“The leaf made starch, and iodine helps reveal it.”

The simulation should connect:

sunlight,
photosynthesis,
starch formation,
chemical testing,
visible evidence.
Overall Visual Setup

The virtual lab appears as a realistic animated biology workstation containing:

fresh green leaves,
test tubes,
boiling water bath,
ethanol container,
forceps,
iodine dropper,
petri dishes,
microscope/zoom controls,
animated heating equipment.

The workspace should feel alive:

steam rises,
liquids ripple,
burners flicker,
glass reflects light subtly.
Step-by-Step Interactive Experience
STEP 1 — Selecting the Leaf

Students choose a leaf:

healthy green leaf,
partially covered leaf,
pale leaf,
dark-stored leaf.

The leaf should appear highly detailed:

visible veins,
texture,
realistic coloration.

Hovering can show:

chlorophyll regions,
sunlight exposure zones.
STEP 2 — Heating in Water

Students drag the leaf into boiling water.

Animation Details

When heated:

water bubbles actively,
steam rises,
leaf softens slightly,
leaf color dulls a little.

A microscopic zoom panel can show:

cells slowing metabolic activity,
proteins denaturing.

The simulation explains:
“Boiling stops chemical reactions in the leaf.”

STEP 3 — Heating in Alcohol

This is the MOST visually important step.

Students place the leaf into ethanol inside a hot water bath.

Key Visual Transformation

The green chlorophyll gradually dissolves out of the leaf.

Important animations:
green pigment swirling into ethanol,
leaf slowly turning pale/yellow-white,
diffusion clouds spreading in liquid.

This moment must feel visually magical.

Students should think:

“The green color is being removed!”

Why This Matters

Many students memorize:
“remove chlorophyll”

without understanding WHY.

The simulation should visually show:

chlorophyll masks starch test results,
removing green pigment reveals the iodine reaction clearly.
STEP 4 — Rinsing the Leaf

Students rinse the brittle leaf in warm water.

Animation
leaf softens again,
texture becomes flexible,
water droplets appear realistically.

This step helps students understand preparation, not just procedure.

STEP 5 — Adding Iodine Solution

This is the emotional payoff moment.

Students use a dropper to place iodine onto the leaf.

Critical Visual Effect

Where starch exists:

iodine spreads,
the leaf rapidly changes from brown/yellow to deep blue-black.

The transition should:

animate smoothly,
spread organically through leaf tissues,
feel chemically reactive.

If no starch exists:

no blue-black appears.
